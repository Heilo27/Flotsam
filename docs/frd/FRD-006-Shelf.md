# FRD-006 — Shelf

**Status:** Draft · 2026-11-16 · Constitution: [TRUTHS.md](../TRUTHS.md) · PRD: [PRD.md](../PRD.md) (feature F6) · Errors: [FRD-000](FRD-000-ErrorSystem.md)
**Owns:** the Shelf panel (reveal, hide, placement, contents), drag-out, drop-in.
**Does not own:** item-to-pasteboard representations and the paste sequence ([FRD-003](FRD-003-Library.md) FR-003-15); classification, kinds and dedupe of what is dropped ([FRD-001](FRD-001-Capture.md)); storage, limits and the ingest pipeline ([FRD-007](FRD-007-RetentionStorage.md)); the toggle hotkey binding ([FRD-005](FRD-005-ShortcutsTriggers.md) FR-005-01, FR-005-07); the settings screen ([FRD-008](FRD-008-OnboardingSettings.md)); pin and delete ([FRD-004](FRD-004-Organize.md)).

## Truth Test
- Serves: S3 — drag is a first-class path: drag clips out to any app, drop files and text in.
- Supports: P1 — for moments when the mouse is already in hand, a clip is one drag away without opening the Library.
- Parity additions, 2026-11-19. Edge bar (FR-006-37, TASK-549): serves P1 (a visible target to rest the pointer on) and stays inside S5 by construction: off by default, no timer, no polling, one window-server tracking area per bar; Ryan's ruling: keep it only if it is cheap and opt-in. Share zone (FR-006-42, TASK-551): serves S3 (a dragged clip can go straight to the system share picker) and stays inside A1 and A4 by construction: the picker and the service the person chooses carry the content at their own action, and Flotsam adds no network code, account, sync, timer or poll; Ryan ruled it into v1 on 2026-11-19, so it is not an escalation.
- Violates: none. The Shelf is not a screenshot tool (A3), not a sync surface (A1), and makes no network call (A4).
- Verdict: KEEP. S3's own falsifier says "Shelf usage near zero after a month of dogfooding → demote to off-by-default". The PRD already ships it on in v1, so this FRD keeps it on by default and provides a way to measure use locally (FR-006-18) so the demotion decision is evidence-based. Sequencing note for Friday: build after F3 and F7, before F5 typed triggers; the Shelf can be cut or delayed without touching any other FRD.

## 1. Purpose
A small panel at a screen edge (or under the menu-bar icon, or hung from the menu bar at the top centre of the screen) that shows pinned and recent clips as draggable cards, lets any card be dragged into any app, and lets files, text and images be dropped onto it to save them. A sticky Shelf also has a search field and type pills to find a clip in the history without opening the Library (FR-006-22). An opt-in collapsed bar at the edge shows where the Shelf is and opens it on hover (FR-006-37).

## 2. User Stories
- As a user writing a document, I drag the screenshot I took ten minutes ago straight from the Shelf into it.
- As a user, I drag a file from Finder onto the Shelf to keep it handy and drag it back out into a mail message later.
- As a user, I drag three files from the Shelf into a chat window and all three arrive.
- As a user, I pin a clip and it is always in the Shelf's first section.
- As a keyboard user, I open the Shelf with its hotkey, arrow to a clip and press Return to paste it.
- As a user who finds the Shelf unnecessary, I turn it off and Flotsam behaves as if it was never there.

## 3. Functional Requirements

**Enable and settings**
- **FR-006-01** Enablement and settings. `shelf.enabled` (default on) turns the Shelf on or off. `shelf.placement` is one of `rightEdge` (default), `leftEdge`, `bottomEdge`, `menuBar`, `topCenter` (a horizontal row of cards hung from the menu bar at the top centre of the screen). `shelf.revealOnEdgeHover` (default off): reveal when the pointer rests in the edge zone without a drag (for `topCenter`, the top-edge zone). `shelf.edgeBar` (default off): show the collapsed edge bar (FR-006-37). Settings keys and their screen are in the FRD-008 registry; defaults and meanings are owned here. When `shelf.enabled` is off: no panel exists, no collapsed bar exists, no global event monitor is installed, the toggle hotkey is unregistered (FR-005-07), the menu bar "Toggle Shelf" item is hidden, and drops onto the menu-bar icon are not accepted.

**Window behavior**
- **FR-006-02** Window. The Shelf is a borderless, non-activating `NSPanel` (`nonactivatingPanel`); it can become key only while sticky (FR-006-04) and a transient reveal never takes key, window level `.floating`, joining all Spaces and permitted over full-screen apps (`canJoinAllSpaces`, `fullScreenAuxiliary`), with no Dock icon or app switcher entry, and excluded from window cycling. Revealing the Shelf never changes the frontmost application, never moves keyboard focus, and never changes the key window of the target app, except as stated in FR-006-08.
- **FR-006-03** Reveal triggers. The Shelf reveals when: (a) a drag is in progress anywhere on the system (the drag pasteboard's `changeCount` changed while a mouse button is down, observed with a global monitor of `leftMouseDragged` events) and the pointer dwells in the edge zone for 300 ms (edge placements only); (b) `shelf.revealOnEdgeHover` is on, no mouse button is down, and the pointer dwells in the edge zone for 500 ms (edge placements only); (c) the Toggle Shelf hotkey or the menu bar "Toggle Shelf" item is used (all placements); (d) for `menuBar` placement, a drag hovers over the menu-bar icon for 300 ms; (e) `shelf.edgeBar` is on and the pointer dwells on the collapsed bar for 300 ms with no button down (edge placements only), or a drag does (FR-006-37). The edge zone is a 4 pt strip at the placement's screen edge (the physical top edge of the screen for `topCenter`, which is the top row of pixels of the menu bar) covering the middle 60 % of that edge's length, on any connected screen. `topCenter` is an edge placement, so (a) and (b) apply to it; it has no menu-bar-icon drag dwell (d), and hover reveal stays under the same `shelf.revealOnEdgeHover` switch as the other edges. It adds no timer and no observer beyond those already named: an idle `topCenter` Shelf is event-driven like any other. The global monitors needed for (a) and (b) exist only while the Shelf is enabled (the `mouseMoved` monitor only while (b) is on). No key events are ever monitored by the Shelf.
- **FR-006-04** Hide rules. A Shelf revealed by (a), (b), (d) or (e) is "transient": it hides 600 ms after the pointer leaves it (and, for a drag, 600 ms after the drag ends), immediately after a successful drop-out or paste, and on a click outside it. A click is outside it when it lands in another app, or in an ordinary Flotsam window such as the Library or Settings. A click on the menu-bar item, in an alert or sheet (such as the delete confirmation), or on the error notice is not outside it and leaves the Shelf showing. A Shelf revealed by (c) is "sticky": it stays until the hotkey or menu item is used again, its close button is clicked, Esc is pressed while it is key (FR-006-08), or the user pastes from it. Both modes hide when a different Space becomes active, when Flotsam's Library opens (only one Flotsam panel is open at a time) and when the display set changes (FR-006-20). Using the hotkey or menu item while a transient Shelf is visible hides it (the command is a toggle). After any hide while the pointer is still in the edge zone, the Shelf does not reveal again until the pointer has left the zone, so it cannot flicker. With `shelf.revealOnEdgeHover` on, the pointer counts as on a visible `topCenter` Shelf also while it is in the strip between the row and the top of the screen (the menu bar), because the pointer that revealed it rests there; without that the row would hide 600 ms after it appeared. With hover off nothing reports pointer movement inside that strip, so the strip does not count: leaving the row upward starts the 600 ms hide like leaving it in any other direction. A point on the seam between two screens belongs to exactly one screen (the one whose lowest or leftmost edge it is), except the physical last column or row of the desktop, which stays on its own screen. The border pixels of a Shelf's area, including the last pixel row or column of the screen, count as on it. Turning the Shelf off, or changing its placement, hides it.
- **FR-006-05** Placement and geometry. Edge placements attach to the chosen edge of the screen that contains the edge zone that was triggered (for hotkey or menu reveal, the screen containing the pointer), inside that screen's `visibleFrame` so the Dock and menu bar are not covered. Left and right: 280 pt wide, height = content height capped to the visible height minus 40 pt, minimum 160 pt, vertically centred. Bottom: 140 pt tall, width = content width capped to 80 % of the visible width, horizontally centred. `topCenter`: the same size rule as bottom (140 pt tall, width = content width, at least 280 pt and at most 80 % of the visible width) horizontally centred and hung from the top of the screen's `visibleFrame`, so its top edge touches the bottom of the menu bar and the menu bar is not covered; on a display with a camera notch the row sits below the menu bar, so the notch is never covered. `menuBar`: 280 pt wide, anchored under the menu-bar icon (clamped to the screen), height as for left and right. The panel slides in over 150 ms from its edge (for `topCenter`, down from under the menu bar) and out over 120 ms; with Reduce Motion on it fades (100 ms) instead of sliding. The Shelf's size and position are not user-adjustable in v1.

**Contents**
- **FR-006-06** Contents and order. Two sections, in order: Pinned (all pinned items, up to 20, ordered by `lastCopiedAt` descending), then Recent (the 20 most recent unpinned items by `lastCopiedAt`, descending). Pinned is read with the pinned filter; Recent is read from the first 200-row page of the unfiltered query with pinned items removed, so Recent holds fewer than 20 only if pinned items fill that page. A section with no items is hidden; with neither, the empty state shows the catalogue text `ui.shelf.empty`. Unless the Shelf's own search or type filter is active (FR-006-22), the Shelf ignores the Library's filter and sort. The list is read when the Shelf is about to show (FR-001-02 `captureNow` runs first) and refreshes live while visible within 200 ms of any capture, pin, edit or delete. Items whose source application is on the ignored-apps list are hidden from both sections (FRD-008 FR-008-42).
- **FR-006-07** Cards. Each item is a card: for `image` and `screenshot` a thumbnail (FRD-007 FR-007-07, shared cache with the Library, FRD-003 FR-003-13); for `file`, `folder` and `bundle` the file icon (bundle: stacked icon with a member count); for text-bearing kinds the first four lines of text (up to 120 characters) with the kind icon; for `color` a large swatch with the value. Every card has a header row: the source app's own icon (16 pt, in colour, never a white-filled glyph; a generic app icon when the source is unknown), the source app's name, a pin glyph on pinned items, and the age (the Library's relative time, FRD-003 FR-003-21) at the trailing edge; the content area sits below the header, so a thumbnail fills it edge to edge (clipped to the card's corners, never overflowing it and never overlapped by a label). A card is a raised surface in light and dark appearance (a filled card with a hairline border and a soft shadow; in dark appearance the hairline is brighter, 1 pt, so the card separates from the panel; the selected card is drawn with a 2 pt accent ring and no tint or fill over the card). Vertical placements stack cards 96 pt tall, except that a text-bearing card is only as tall as its text needs (its header and its lines of text, 1 to 4, measured with the font and width the card draws the text in, so no blank line is left under two or three lines; never under 48 pt, which only a one-line card reaches, and never over 96 pt; image, screenshot, file, folder, bundle and colour cards stay 96 pt), and the panel height follows the cards' real heights (hill-climb r2 and r3, TASK-529); the bottom and top-centre placements lay cards out as one horizontal row of cards 144 pt wide, which scrolls horizontally when the cards are wider than the panel (the Pinned items first, then the Recent ones, without section headers). Cards show no notes or tags. Missing file members show the Missing badge as the Library does (FRD-003). A card's Missing state is the item row's own `isMissing`, read from the same query the Library uses (FRD-003 FR-003-07), so the Shelf has no Missing rule of its own and the two can never disagree. A card's text is read through a Shelf port served by one storage query (the stored text, cut in the query); if that read fails the cards show their item's primary line and the failure is reported like a failed list read (STO-DB-004).

**Interaction**
- **FR-006-08** Selection and keyboard (sticky mode only). A sticky Shelf may become key (the app stays inactive; the target app stays frontmost, as for the Library, FRD-003 FR-003-01). When the Shelf is revealed its first card (the first Pinned card, else the first Recent card) is selected, so Down, Down, Return pastes the third card. While key: arrow keys move the selection through the cards and stop at the first and last card (they do not wrap), Return pastes the selected card into the recorded target app (the frontmost application when the Shelf was revealed) by the FRD-003 FR-003-15 sequence and hides the Shelf, ⌥Return pastes as plain text, ⇧Return and ⌘C both mean copy only (the same action), Space is not used, Esc hides (a sticky Shelf with a search or type filter active clears it first, FR-006-22). Return, ⌥Return, ⇧Return and ⌘C act only when exactly one card is selected; with no card or several cards selected they do nothing. A transient Shelf never becomes key. Accessibility denied: Return behaves as ⇧Return with `SET-PRM-001` (FRD-003 FR-003-17).
- **FR-006-09** Mouse. A click selects a card (⌘-click toggles, ⇧-click extends the selection). Double-click pastes the card under the pointer (never the whole selection) into the recorded target app by the FR-003-15 sequence and hides the Shelf. The context menu (right-click) offers, in order: Paste, Paste as Plain Text, Copy, separator, Pin or Unpin (FRD-004 FR-004-01), separator, Delete (FRD-004 FR-004-09, with its confirmation rules). No other editing actions exist in the Shelf. Menu actions are scoped by the clicked card (the card the right-click landed on): Paste, Paste as Plain Text and Copy always act on the clicked card alone. Pin or Unpin and Delete act on the whole selection when the selection contains the clicked card, and otherwise on the clicked card alone. Dragging follows the same selection rule (FR-006-10).

**Drag out**
- **FR-006-10** Drag out. Dragging a card starts a standard drag session after the system drag threshold. If the card is part of the selection, the whole selection is dragged; if it is outside the selection, that card alone is dragged and the selection is unchanged. The drag provides the same representations the Library writes on paste (FRD-003 FR-003-15 step 2: stored text representations; image data through `NSImage`; file URLs for file, folder and bundle kinds) as drag-pasteboard items, lazily provided; a bundle contributes one pasteboard item per existing member; a multi-selection contributes the items in on-screen order. The allowed operation is copy only (`.copy`), never move or link, so an original file is never moved or renamed by a drop. Library rows drag out through the same session (FRD-003 FR-003-45), so these rules and FR-006-11 and FR-006-21 hold there too, with the Library's own selection. The drag image is the card (several cards: a stacked image with a count badge). Variables are not expanded (FRD-005 FR-005-17). The drag never writes the general pasteboard and so is never captured as a clipboard change; nothing is marked own-write.
- **FR-006-11** Drag-out accounting and failures. When the drag ends with an accepted operation, each dragged item's `pasteCount` is incremented and `lastUsedAt` set (FRD-007), and a transient Shelf hides. `pasteCount` counts cards, not pasteboard items: a bundle dragged out counts once however many members it contributes. If a bundle or file item has missing members, they are skipped. In a multi-card drag, a card with nothing to drag is skipped and the other cards still drag; `LIB-DAT-001` shows a HUD and the drag does not start only when every dragged card has nothing to drag. A card has nothing to drag when it is a bundle or file item with no remaining members, or an image with no usable image data. If the drag list is empty, the drag does not start and `SHF-OS-001` shows a HUD. If the drag cannot start (the card is not in a window, or there is no mouse event to start from) or the system cannot supply a card's data (it refuses the card's pasteboard item, or asks for a type the card does not hold), `SHF-OS-001` shows a HUD and nothing changes; when only some cards fail that way the others still drag. A file that has gone missing or cannot be read by the time the drag starts is skipped like any missing member. A drag cancelled by the user (Esc, or dropped on nothing) changes nothing and shows nothing; a drag that has started and ends with no drop is always treated as a cancel.

**Drop in**
- **FR-006-12** Drop targets and acceptance. The panel and (for `menuBar` placement) the menu-bar icon accept drops. While a drag hovers, the drag pasteboard is inspected for type identifiers only (no data read): if it holds at least one honoured type (FRD-001 FR-001-05) and no marker type from FRD-001 FR-001-04 gate 3 (`ConcealedType`, `TransientType`, `AutoGeneratedType`), the Shelf shows a highlighted drop zone and the cursor shows the copy badge; otherwise the drop is refused (no highlight, not-allowed cursor). Drags that originate in the Shelf itself are never accepted: a card dragged out of the Shelf and dropped back on it is refused silently, with no HUD. A drag that began in a Library row (FRD-003 FR-003-45) is not the Shelf's own and follows the rules above like any other app's drag.
- **FR-006-13** Drop processing. A drop is processed exactly as if its pasteboard content had been a pasteboard change: classification, kinds, typed-text detection, bundle semantics, `contentKey` and dedupe (FRD-001 FR-001-05 to -15) and then the ingest pipeline (FRD-007 FR-007-05). Differences, all deliberate: (a) it is an explicit user action, so the capture mode (gate 1) and the ignored-apps list (gate 4) do not apply, so a drop works in Paused and Curated modes, and a drop from an ignored application is stored with no source bundle identifier (the name is kept) so the ignored-apps hide never applies to it and it is listed in the Library and the Shelf (FRD-008 FR-008-42); (b) the Concealed, Transient and AutoGenerated types still reject (gate 3), with `SHF-VAL-001`; (c) the source app is the frontmost application at drop time (the app the drag came from); (d) a dropped item that already exists bumps `copyCount` and `lastCopiedAt` (FRD-001 FR-001-15) and moves to the top of Recent; if that item's source is on the ignored-apps list its source bundle identifier is cleared by the same bump, so it is visible and highlighted (FRD-008 FR-008-42). Several files dropped together become one bundle (FRD-001 FR-001-12), whatever `capture.autoBundle` says (that setting governs copies only); folders are stored as references; nothing dropped is copied or moved on disk except what the storage pipeline stores for text and images.
- **FR-006-14** Drop errors. A file that cannot be read or no longer exists → skipped, `SHF-IO-001`, other files in the same drop continue; files and folders are held by reference and are never size-checked, so a file of any size that can be read is stored (FR-007-06); `SHF-LIM-001` with the limit applies only to dropped text over 10 MiB or dropped image data over 100 MiB, which is skipped, and `IngestLimits` raises it (FRD-007 FR-007-06); a drop with no honoured type or only ignored types (file promises, colours, PDFs, other `dyn.*`, FRD-001 FR-001-06) → refused, `SHF-VAL-001`; dropped image data that cannot be decoded (FR-007-07) → stores nothing and is refused the same way, `SHF-VAL-001`, never the capture code `CAP-VAL-001`, which is log-only, so the person always sees the refusal. HUD throttling follows FR-000-08. A drop that has at least one stored result is a success and shows no error unless some part was skipped. Trimming a bundle to the 500-member cap is reported by CAP-LIM-003 and does not count as skipped, so a drop trimmed that way and otherwise fully stored is still a success here.
- **FR-006-15** Drop feedback. A successful drop briefly highlights the new or bumped card (600 ms) and scrolls it into view at the top of Recent; the transient Shelf stays visible for 1.5 s after the drop, then follows FR-006-04. The hold applies only while the Shelf is visible: a drop that finishes storing after the Shelf has hidden does not show it again and leaves no hold behind, so the next reveal follows FR-006-04 from its own start. The drop never changes the frontmost application.

**Cross-cutting**
- **FR-006-16** Pasteboard interplay. Pastes from the Shelf (FR-006-08, FR-006-09) write the general pasteboard with own-write markers (FRD-001 FR-001-03) and so are never re-captured. Drag-out and drop-in use the drag pasteboard only.
- **FR-006-17** Performance. From the trigger (dwell complete, hotkey, or menu item) to the panel on screen with its content (ordered front, sections loaded and drawn, the slide or fade under way): p95 ≤ 120 ms over 20 runs with 10,000 items (`shelf.reveal` signpost, FRD-000 FR-000-12). The span ends when the motion begins, not when it finishes: the slide-in is 150 ms (FR-006-05), which alone is over the budget, so counting it would make the budget unreachable (TASK-556: the signpost read p50 164 ms against 37 to 62 ms by external timing because it ended with the 150 ms slide). The dwell is not part of the span. Drag start (past threshold) to drag image visible: p95 ≤ 100 ms. Drop of a 5 MiB image to the card visible in the Recent section: p95 ≤ 800 ms. With the Shelf enabled and idle, there are no timers and the only active observer is the `leftMouseDragged` global monitor, averaging under 0.5 % of one CPU core during continuous dragging. The collapsed bar adds no timer and no monitor: its only event source is a window-server tracking area that fires when the pointer enters or leaves it (FR-006-37). Thumbnails share the 64 MB cache from FRD-003 FR-003-13.
- **FR-006-18** Local usage evidence for S3. Each reveal (by trigger type), drag-out (card count) and drop-in (outcome) writes one Info log line containing counts and the outcome code only (FR-000-11, FR-000-10): `shelf.reveal` carries the trigger type (`drag`, `hover`, `hotkey`, `menuItem` or `menuBarIconDrag`), `shelf.dragOut` the card count (a bundle counts once, as in FR-006-11), `shelf.dropIn` the outcome and, when not every item was stored, the error code. Trimming a bundle to the 500-member cap is reported by CAP-LIM-003 and does not count as "not every item was stored", so it adds no error code to the line. The FRD-000 `LogEvent.shelf` case was extended with that optional detail for this. There is no persistence, counter or network call; the S3 falsifier review counts lines with `log show` during dogfooding. No file name, text or app name is logged.
- **FR-006-19** Accessibility and display. Every card has a VoiceOver label in the Library's form (FRD-003 FR-003-21) with actions Paste, Copy, Pin; drag-out has the VoiceOver action "Drag" replaced by the Paste action (a drag has no equivalent). The panel respects Reduce Motion (FR-006-05), Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast, and uses the system accent for the drop-zone highlight. The panel adopts light or dark appearance from the system. Text scales with the system's text-size settings where the platform provides them.
- **FR-006-20** Screen changes. If the displays change while the Shelf is visible, it hides; hot zones are recalculated for the new screen set within 1 s. A screen with no usable visible frame for the chosen edge (for example fully covered) falls back to the screen containing the pointer for hotkey reveals and has no hot zone otherwise.
- **FR-006-22** Search and type filter (sticky mode only). A sticky Shelf shows, above its cards, a search field and a row of type pills: All, then one pill per item type that has at least one item, named and ordered as the Library's Types sidebar (FRD-003 FR-003-12), which includes its Settings > Types list (`library.types`, FRD-008 FR-008-26): a type switched off there has no pill, and the list is read at each reveal. The field shows a clear button (`ui.btn.clearSearch`, "Clear Search", with the same tooltip and accessibility label) only while it has text; it empties the field as Esc does. A pill is never drawn cut off (hill-climb r3, TASK-529): the pills that fit the panel's width are shown whole, All first, and the rest are in a trailing "More" pill (`ui.shelf.moreTypes`) that opens a menu of those types with the active one checked, so every type stays reachable; when all pills fit there is no More pill. The panel's own close button (top right, tooltip `ui.shelf.close`) is a bare cross, not a circled one, so it is never mistaken for the field's clear button; it is always shown, the clear button only while the field has text. In the vertical placements the field is above the pills; in the bottom and top-centre strips they share one 32 pt row and the cards are that much shorter. A transient Shelf shows neither and never takes key (FR-006-08). Each reveal starts with an empty field and All selected, and the field has key focus. Matching is the Library's, not a second matcher: the text goes through FRD-003 FR-003-09 (tokens ANDed; plain text, link, note, tag names, source app, member names and kind label all searched) and the pill is the Library's single type filter, in the one query the Library runs (FRD-007 FR-007-18). Results are read from the whole history, not only the newest 20: while a search or type filter is active the Shelf shows up to one 200-row page of matches in the same two sections (pinned matches under Pinned, the rest under Recent; the horizontal strips show them as one row, Pinned first), newest first. Typing or choosing a pill re-reads on each change with no debounce, selects the first match, and scrolls to the start; the list keeps refreshing live (FR-006-06). A search or filter with no match shows the Library's "No matches" text (`ui.library.noResultsTitle`, `ui.library.noResultsBody`), not the empty-history text. Keyboard: typing goes to the field; Up and Down move through the cards and Return, ⌥Return and ⇧Return paste or copy as in FR-006-08; ⌥Up and ⌥Down move to the previous or next pill and stop at All and at the last pill; Left and Right belong to the field while it has text and move the card selection while it is empty; ⌘C copies the field's selected text when it has a selection, else the selected card; Esc clears the search text, then (next press) the type filter, then (next press) hides the Shelf. An input method composing text keeps every key until it commits. The field and pills use the Library's catalogue keys (`ui.library.searchPlaceholder`, `ui.library.sidebarAll`, `ui.library.type*`). There is no tag pill, no source-app pill and no setting of its own (the pills follow the Library's `library.types`): tags are found by typing their name, and the feature adds nothing to FRD-008 beyond that shared list. Search runs in the query layer, so it meets the 100 ms budget at 10,000 items (FRD-007 FR-007-18) with no Shelf-side matching, timer or cache, and an idle Shelf still has no timer (FR-006-17).
- **FR-006-32** Loading and searching state (parity ticket TASK-546, 2026-11-19). A reveal, search or type-filter read that is still running 150 ms after it started shows a small spinner with the caption `ui.library.loading` (or `ui.library.searching` while the field has text or a pill is chosen), the Library's keys by name: centred in the panel when no cards are on screen yet, else as a badge over the top of the cards, which stay. A read that finishes in under 150 ms shows no spinner, and while a read runs with no cards the panel is blank, never `ui.shelf.empty` or the no-matches text. The delay is `BusyIndicator.delay`, the constant the Library shares (FRD-003 FR-003-50), and the same one-shot task: armed when the read starts, cancelled when it finishes, fails or the Shelf hides. It adds no repeating timer, and an idle Shelf still has none (FR-006-17). The Shelf has no tag filter (FR-006-22), so it has no empty-tag state.
- **FR-006-21** Secure cards. A secure item (FRD-001 FR-001-25) shows as a lock with the fixed mask, never its text, and an "Unavailable" badge when its Keychain key cannot be read (FRD-003 FR-003-23). Pasting or copying it from the Shelf follows FRD-003 FR-003-23. Dragging it out asks the device owner to authenticate at mouse-down, once per secure card and every time: its text is decrypted only after that and is offered on the drag pasteboard as plain text with `ConcealedType`. A secure card whose check is cancelled or fails (`LIB-OS-002`), or whose key is missing (`LIB-DAT-002`, no prompt), is skipped like a card with nothing to drag; if no card remains, no drag starts and the error shows. If the mouse button is released while the prompt is up, no drag starts.
- **FR-006-37** Collapsed edge bar (parity ticket TASK-549, 2026-11-19). With `shelf.edgeBar` on (default off), the Shelf is enabled and the placement is an edge placement (`rightEdge`, `leftEdge`, `bottomEdge`, `topCenter`), each connected screen shows a small pill on the placement's edge of its `visibleFrame`: 6 pt thick and 96 pt long (shorter if the edge is), centred along the edge, in the system secondary label colour, with an accessibility label (`ui.shelf.edgeBar`). It is a borderless non-activating panel that never becomes key or main, never changes the frontmost app, and floats on all Spaces like the Shelf (FR-006-02). Resting the pointer on it for 300 ms with no button down, or dragging onto it for 300 ms, reveals the Shelf by the normal reveal path (FR-006-03e, drag via FR-006-03a), so the hide rules, the no-reflicker rule and the one-panel rule (FR-006-04) are unchanged. While the Shelf is visible the bars are hidden; they return when it hides. The bar is not shown for the `menuBar` placement, and not at all while the setting is off or the Shelf is disabled. Idle budget (TRUTHS S5; ten minutes idle with the bar on: at most 0.2 % CPU, 2.5 wake-ups per second, 60 MB): the bar has no timer, no polling and no monitor of its own; hover comes from one tracking area per bar, which the window server drives, so an idle machine with the bar showing does no work. The bars are rebuilt only when the setting, the placement, the screen set or the Shelf's visibility changes.
- **FR-006-42** Share zone (parity ticket TASK-551). While a drag of Flotsam's own clips is over the panel, whether it began on a card of this Shelf or on a Library row (FRD-003 FR-003-45), the Shelf draws a share zone labelled "Drop to share" (`ui.shelf.share`): a 64 pt strip along the trailing edge of the bottom and top-centre placements and along the bottom of the left, right and menu-bar placements (`ShelfLayout.shareZone`). The zone exists only for the length of such a drag: it is not drawn otherwise, and a drag from another app never shows it. Over the zone the highlight strengthens and the drop is taken; dropping on the zone does not store a clip and does not pin one. A drag from this Shelf's own cards that is not over the zone is still refused silently (FR-006-12), and a Library drag that is not over the zone is a drop-in as before (FR-006-13). On a drop on the zone the app opens the system share picker (`NSSharingServicePicker`) anchored on the zone, with the items the drag would have dropped: the Shelf adds no second item builder, the picker receives the drag session's own pasteboard writers (FR-006-10, FRD-003 FR-003-45), so a card that provides nothing is skipped exactly as in a drag, an image is shared as an image and a secure card as plain text. A secure card authenticates first and every time, because the drag that carries it already asked at mouse-down (FR-006-21): there is no path to the picker that skips the check, and a card whose check fails is not shared. The drop counts as an accepted drag-out (FR-006-11, FR-006-18: one paste per card, one `shelf.dragOut` line with the card count); unlike another accepted drop it does not hide a transient Shelf, and a transient Shelf stays up for as long as the picker is open, since the pointer is then on the picker and not on the panel (the reveal machine's `sharing` event, FR-006-04). When the person has chosen a service or dismissed the picker, the usual hide rules apply again. Flotsam adds no network code: the picker and the service the person chooses (AirDrop, Messages, Mail) carry the content, at that action, and the build gate that bans network APIs in the sources (FRD-000) is unchanged. There is no timer, poll or observer for the zone or the picker (FR-006-17); it is drawn from the drag-destination callbacks the panel already gets, and the picker is held only while it is open.
- **FR-006-47** Transient Shelf hides on the first exit (bug ticket TASK-557, 2026-11-19). A transient Shelf that appeared under a pointer that has not moved since (the usual case: the pointer rests in the edge zone, or on the collapsed bar or menu-bar icon, and the panel appears under it) hides 600 ms after the pointer first leaves it, exactly as one the pointer moved into does (FR-006-04). The panel's pointer tracking area is told at each reveal whether the pointer is already inside the panel's frame, so the window server reports that first exit; a tracking area is otherwise only told the pointer is inside once it has seen it move in, and a pointer that left without that got no report. The flag is cleared when the pointer leaves and when the panel hides. It adds no timer and no monitor (FR-006-17).
- **FR-006-48** Cards' VoiceOver age stays current (bug ticket TASK-528, 2026-11-19). While the Shelf is open, the age in each card's VoiceOver label (FRD-003 FR-003-21) advances without any other input, by the Library's rule and no second one: FRD-003 FR-003-24 (the refresh is due when the soonest card's label would read differently, one step at a time, calendar days, future dates, nothing for copies more than 7 days old). All cards are labelled from one reading of one clock per refresh, the same clock the cards use when the Shelf opens. The same age is drawn on each card's header (FR-006-07), driven by this same refresh, so the visible age and the VoiceOver age never disagree. While the Shelf is hidden nothing is scheduled (FR-006-17), and revealing it reads the clock afresh. The refresh is one sleeping task that runs only while the Shelf is open and is cancelled on every hide; it is not a repeating timer and adds no monitor.

## 4. Data Requirements
- Reads items, pinned flag, thumbnails and `lastCopiedAt` (FRD-007); writes only through ingest (drops), `pasteCount`/`lastUsedAt` (drag-out and paste) and FRD-004 actions (pin, delete).
- Settings (registry in FRD-008): `shelf.enabled` (bool, default true), `shelf.placement` (enum, default `rightEdge`), `shelf.revealOnEdgeHover` (bool, default false), `shelf.edgeBar` (bool, default false).
- Constants (one home, here): edge zone 4 pt by 60 %; dwell 300 ms (drag), 500 ms (hover) and 300 ms (collapsed bar); collapsed bar 6 pt by 96 pt; hide delay 600 ms; sections 20 and 20; card height 96 pt; width 280 pt; bottom and top-centre height 140 pt, row card width 144 pt.
- Catalogue strings: `ui.shelf.empty`, `ui.shelf.drop` (drop-zone label), `ui.shelf.close`, `ui.shelf.edgeBar` (the bar's accessibility label), `ui.shelf.sectionRecent`, `ui.shelf.menuPin`, `ui.shelf.menuUnpin`, `ui.shelf.menuDelete`, `ui.shelf.missing`, `ui.shelf.share` (share-zone label, FR-006-42), `ui.shelf.moreTypes` ("More", the type-pill overflow menu, FR-006-22). Paste, Paste as Plain Text, Copy and the Pinned label reuse the Library's keys; the Delete confirmation text is FRD-004's.

## 5. Business Rules
- Dragging out never moves, renames or deletes an original file; the allowed operation is copy.
- A drop is a capture: same kinds, same dedupe, same limits. It ignores capture mode and ignored apps because the user acted explicitly, but never defeats Concealed, Transient or AutoGenerated markers.
- Revealing the Shelf never steals focus from the app the user is working in.
- The Shelf never reads clip data merely to decide whether it can accept a drag.
- Turning the Shelf off removes every observer it installed.

## 6. Error States (codes in FRD-000 §7)
| Situation | Code | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Drag session could not start, the drag list was empty, or the system aborted it | SHF-OS-001 | cancelled; HUD |
| Dropped file unreadable or gone | SHF-IO-001 | file skipped; HUD |
| Dropped text over 10 MiB or dropped image data over 100 MiB (files are held by reference and never size-checked, FR-007-06) | SHF-LIM-001 | item skipped; HUD |
| Drop has no storable content, or carries Concealed/Transient/AutoGenerated types | SHF-VAL-001 | refused; HUD |
| Every dragged card has nothing to drag (all members missing, or an image with no usable data) | LIB-DAT-001 | drag not started; HUD |
| Secure card dragged and the owner check cancelled or failed / key missing | LIB-OS-002 / LIB-DAT-002 | that card skipped; if none remain no drag starts; HUD (FR-006-21) |
| Accessibility off when pasting from a sticky Shelf | SET-PRM-001 | copy-only; HUD |
| Database write failed on drop | STO-DB-001 | rolled back; surfaced per FRD-000 |

## 9. Assumptions and open items (flagged for Friday)
1. Reveal model. The PRD says "edge/menu-bar panel" and does not define the reveal. This FRD reveals on a system-wide drag reaching the edge (so scrollbars and the Dock are not disturbed), on a hotkey or menu item, and optionally on edge hover. Whether a drag in progress can be reliably detected with global monitors without Accessibility on macOS 26 is unverified and needs a spike before build; fallback is hotkey, menu and hover only.
2. Drops ignore capture mode and ignored apps (FR-006-13a) because they are explicit. This is a product choice; Concealed and Transient types still reject.
3. Source app of a drop is the frontmost application (FR-006-13c); drag sources outside the frontmost app (rare) are mislabelled.
4. Click behaviour: double-click pastes. A single click only selects, to avoid accidental pastes while moving to drag.
5. Dock overlap is avoided by using the visible frame; a Dock on the same edge is accepted as is.
6. On by default in v1 as the PRD says; the S3 review after a month of dogfooding uses the local log lines (FR-006-18). Demotion to off-by-default is a one-line default change in FR-006-01 and the FRD-008 registry.
7. A failed card-text read is reported (HUD, STO-DB-004) rather than silently degrading, because the same database just answered the list query and a second failure is worth surfacing once. The FRD is otherwise silent; this is the conservative reading and is one line to change in the model.
8. Wired scope (S3 slice 3, 2026-11-17). The app builds the Shelf and a person can use it: the menu-bar item "Toggle Shelf" (FR-006-03c) and the toggle hotkey (default from FRD-005 FR-005-01, shown only while the Shelf is enabled) reveal a sticky Shelf on the screen with the pointer, in the placement chosen in Settings; the panel never takes focus from the app being pasted into, takes keyboard focus only while sticky, and hides on paste, close button, Esc, a Space or display change, opening the Library, or turning the Shelf off or changing its placement; showing the Shelf in turn hides an open Library (FR-006-04, one Flotsam panel at a time). Each reveal writes its `shelf.reveal` Info line (trigger `hotkey` or `menuItem`, FR-006-18) and the `shelf.reveal` signpost runs from the trigger to the panel fully visible (FR-006-17). Cards, sections, Missing badges (the same rule and the same query as the Library, FR-006-07), paste, copy, pin and delete behave as specified; deleting asks for confirmation first. The pointer-leave timer runs only while a transient Shelf is showing (FR-006-17). Also wired (S3 slices 4 and 5, 2026-11-17): the three drag-edge and hover reveals (FR-006-03 a, b, d) from global mouse-position, button and drag-pasteboard monitors that exist only while the Shelf is enabled (the mouse-moved monitor only while edge hover is on) plus a tracking area and drag-destination callbacks on the panel for the onShelf region; Esc and a click outside hide as FR-006-04 says; drag-out (FR-006-10, 11) and drop-in through the capture classifier (FR-006-12 to 14) with the drop feedback and 1.5 s hold (FR-006-15); and the `menuBar` placement anchored under the menu-bar icon (FR-006-05c). Known limits: a drag the system refuses over the panel is refused by the system, so SHF-VAL-001 cannot be shown for it; a drop directly onto the menu-bar icon is not taken (the Shelf appears under the icon after the 300 ms dwell and accepts the drop); hover and edge reveal do not see the pointer over this app's own windows.
9. Top-centre placement (parity ticket TASK-533, 2026-11-19). `topCenter` is added as a fifth placement so the quick-access row can hang from the menu bar. It reuses the bottom strip's card row, size and width rule (one geometry home, `ShelfLayout`), the existing hover switch and the existing hide rules. Open product question for Friday: with `shelf.revealOnEdgeHover` off (the default) the top row opens by drag or hotkey only, exactly like every other edge; making `topCenter` open on hover without the switch is a one-line change to the hover rule in FR-006-03 and the reveal machine. Not verifiable without a physical display: the pointer reaching the top row of pixels over a notched or auto-hiding menu bar, and mouse-wheel scrolling of the row.
10. Search and type filter (parity ticket TASK-534, 2026-11-19). The Shelf gains a search field and type pills (FR-006-22), only while sticky, because a transient Shelf must never take key (FR-006-08). The pill is the Library's single `LibraryFilter.kind`, so one type at a time and no tag or source-app pill; a tag is found by typing its name (FR-003-09 searches tag names). While filtering, the Shelf reads one 200-row page, not 20 cards, so a search reaches into the history; this is the ticket's call and the one place the cap in FR-006-06 gives way. Written under the project's build-now-test-later order: the AC-006-27 behaviour is built and compiles but is untested.
11. Collapsed edge bar (parity ticket TASK-549, 2026-11-19). Opt-in, off by default, so a person who does not want a mark on their screen never sees one. Size (6 pt by 96 pt), the 300 ms dwell and the system secondary colour are first guesses to tune after dogfooding. The bar is a separate panel per screen, not part of the Shelf panel, so the Shelf's own animation and geometry are untouched. The idle measurement (AC-006-37) is deferred by Ryan's standing order to build first and test later; the design meets S5 by having no work to do while idle. Needs no ADR-0004 amendment because it adds no recurring or idle work.
12. Share zone (parity ticket TASK-551, 2026-11-19). "AirDrop and Share-sheet actions from the Shelf" moves from Out of Scope to FR-006-42: one zone, the system share picker, no Flotsam sharing code. Decisions recorded here: the zone is shown only during a Flotsam clip drag (unobtrusive, and it costs nothing when idle, FR-006-17); both the Shelf's own cards and Library rows can be dropped on it; the picker takes the drag's own writers (no second builder) and a secure card is shared only after the mouse-down authentication, so the owner is asked before any text exists; the drop is counted like any accepted drag-out. A share from the Library's own panel (FRD-003) is still Out of Scope there: the Library gets nothing new; its rows are only a source for this zone. Written under the build-now-test-later order, and not verified on a running Shelf: whether the picker's popover anchors correctly from a non-activating panel, and whether every sharing service reads the lazily provided pasteboard items; both need a run on a physical Mac. If a service cannot read them, the fix is in the share path only (provide the data eagerly for a share).
13. Transient Shelf stuck after reveal (bug ticket TASK-557, 2026-11-19). S2's perf run saw the Shelf stay visible after a transient reveal in 2 of 25 reveals, needing 1 to 5 extra pointer moves to hide (FR-006-47). Cause, from the code and the symptom: with hover off no global monitor reports plain pointer moves, and the panel's tracking area is the only source of the pointer leaving the panel; a panel that appears under a stationary pointer has not seen it enter, so whether the first exit is reported depends on window-server timing.
